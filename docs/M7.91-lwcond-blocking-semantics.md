# M7.91: implement the active guest lwcond path

## Evidence from the M7.90 device run

`790.txt` reports an active AOT entry, 8,803,086 AOT calls and 1,774,377 HLE
calls in 63 seconds. Its latest HLE entry is `sys_lwcond_wait`
(`0x2A6D9D51`), called from `0x004D653C`. SPURS recognizes all 18 registered
policy modules, the C8 policy is image 6, and WWS admissions reach 95,450.
Title job-chain requests, guest draws and presented flips remain zero.

The source ELF disassembly shows the caller at `0x004D6504`. It passes its
condition object at `r3`, calls `sys_lwcond_wait` at `0x004D6538`, returns when
the wait succeeds, and follows a timeout/error path otherwise. Static branch
analysis finds dozens of direct calls to this wait helper in the ELF. The report's
`ponteiro host=NÃO` was also a misleading label: that field is the high-bits
check for the 32-bit guest EA, and `faixa=válida` confirms the PM range passed.

## Functional correction

The pinned `sysPrxForUser` runtime previously made `sys_lwcond_signal`,
`sys_lwcond_signal_all` and `sys_lwcond_signal_to` return success without
waking anyone. `sys_lwcond_wait` released the guest lwmutex, slept for 1 ms,
reacquired the mutex and returned success even without a signal. That is a
polling approximation, not the PS3 condition-variable contract.

M7.91 replaces it with guest-condition wait queues. A waiter is queued under
the host condition lock before its guest lwmutex is released, preventing a lost
signal in that transition. It parks until the matching signal, broadcast,
targeted signal or timeout, then reacquires the lwmutex before returning. The
build patcher fails if the new semantics are absent from the pinned runtime.

This corrects a demonstrated HLE mismatch in the current PPU wait path. It does
not prove that the title now submits a job chain or produces an RSX frame. The
device must still show WWS/job execution followed by guest draw and flip counts
above zero.
