# M7.84: NPUA80918 SPU workload registration

## Inventory from `Pastagow.MOV` and the two manifests

`ARCHIVES` is ASCII/CRLF, declares 19 entries, and contains 19 unique archive
names. `LOCALIZED_ARCHIVES` has the same format and declares eight unique
localized archives. Their byte sizes (264 and 149) match the Files.app view of
`NPUA80918/USRDIR`; that view also shows the folder has 58 items and includes
PSARC files such as `TPPS.PSARC` and the listed language archives.

This confirms the two small sidecar manifests and the visible PSARC inventory.
They are indexes, not archive contents or SPU programs. The iOS preflight
currently counts `.psarc` extensions but does not parse either list; the live
M7.83 report counted guest file calls/bytes but did not record the file path.
The manifests therefore cannot establish that a particular archive was read
by the game.

The Ascension PC launcher reads `LOCALIZED_ARCHIVES` when building language
mods. Its `scripts/unix/relift.sh` also has separate `spu` and `jobmods`
stages: `spu` lifts the four embedded SPU ELFs plus the WWS policy module;
`jobmods` lifts the 33 modules staged in `work/jobmods`. The iOS builder had
compiled the four embedded SPU C files, but had not linked the generated
`spu_workloads.c` registry. `m772_bridge.cpp` called each image's local
function-table registrar only. That does not associate an ELF fingerprint
with a native entry, so SPURS could not dispatch those compiled images. The
M7.83 capture's zero dispatches and zero guest draws are consistent with this
missing link.

## NPUA-specific comparison

The four embedded SPU ELFs were extracted from the supplied NPUA80918 ELF at
the same four offsets and with the same entry points as the source lift:

| Image | Entry | Full ELF FNV-1a64 | Executable text FNV-1a64 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| SPU 0 | `0x3070` | `574361C36D1A4C05` | `136CB3B1B78906DF` |
| SPU 1 | `0x3050` | `F4C2A58BDE92CE5E` | `2114412A6E6691EA` |
| SPU 2 | `0x3070` | `4DA69B4166D997AD` | `D9E0419A06197CF4` |
| SPU 3 | `0x3050` | `00E729804474449A` | `C9B3274B709C201A` |

The PC port's WWS PM recipe extracts 12,288 bytes from `0x77CA80` and expects
fingerprint `C8D774963A96DCF2` for its European executable. The same range in
the supplied NPUA ELF fingerprints as `CE052ACDA7412063`. Reusing the PC
constant would leave the NPUA policy module unresolved. I wrapped the NPUA
bytes as an SPU ELF at LS `0xA00` and lifted them with PS3Recomp. The lift
produced 145 functions and 31 call targets; the generated registry records
both the wrapped ELF fingerprint and the exact NPUA raw-code fingerprint.

## M7.84 integration

- Added the NPUA WWS PM's generated C and header under
  `runtime/m772-npua-spu/wws_pm_npua/`.
- Added `spu_workloads.c`, generated with PS3Recomp's
  `build_spu_workloads.py`. It registers the four existing SPU images and the
  NPUA PM by full-image and text fingerprints, with distinct local-store image
  IDs.
- Changed `m772_bridge.cpp` to call the generated registry once during
  PS3Recomp initialization. The builder compiles both added C units and gates
  the resulting archive/final Mach-O on their symbols.
- The report no longer hardcodes “five SPU images”: at runtime it queries the
  dispatcher for the four NPUA SPU ELF fingerprints and the exact raw WWS PM
  fingerprint, and reports how many lookups resolve to their expected image
  IDs. That is registry evidence only; the existing PM-resolution and dispatch
  counters remain the evidence for actual SPURS execution.
- Updated the next IPA identity to 7.84.0. The uploaded manifests and video are
  removed from the current branch tree after analysis; their Git history is
  preserved.

## What this fixes and what remains

This closes a real integration gap: compiled SPU code now has a fingerprint
route into PS3Recomp's native SPURS dispatcher. The 7.83 report's 0/38 policy
modules, zero dispatches, and zero draws are not evidence that a game frame was
rendered. M7.84 must still be built and run on the iPhone to confirm whether
the NPUA PM resolves and dispatches.

The PC port's separate 33-job-module stage is still not part of the iOS build.
The two manifests only name archives; the video does not provide their bytes
for extraction, and no `patch.sdat`/`data.sdat` module data was uploaded. Those
modules must be found and lifted from the exact NPUA content before claiming a
menu or gameplay frame. The PC port's EU fingerprints cannot substitute for
fingerprints from this NPUA release. The first-frame gate remains a guest RSX
draw followed by a presented guest flip.
